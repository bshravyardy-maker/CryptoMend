Threat Model & Architectural Principles

1. Layered Error Correction & Cryptography (Encode-then-Encrypt with RS Inner/Outer)

A common cryptographic misconception is that encryption alone can repair corrupted data. In reality, modern authenticated ciphers like AES-GCM are deliberately designed to fail completely upon any bit alteration to prevent forgery.

The AREF system establishes a defensible layered boundary:

  • Plaintext Chunking: Segmented into block units bounded by Reed-Solomon Galois Field $GF(2^8)$ maximum codeword limit of 255 symbols.
  • AES-256-GCM Encryption: Each chunk is encrypted with a unique 96-bit nonce derived from master salt + monotonic block counter. Authenticated Associated Data (AAD) strictly binds block index and chunk length.
  • Reed-Solomon Parity Protection: Codewords protect the block header, authentication tag, and ciphertext. RS corrects up to $\lfloor nsym / 2 \rfloor$ unknown symbol errors or up to $nsym$ known erasures.
  • Zero-Trust Authentication Verification: Reconstructed data is never accepted based on RS correction alone. AES-GCM tag verification must pass with 128-bit cryptographic guarantee before data is accepted.

2. Threat Model Assumptions

  • Channel Noise vs Active Adversary: Unintentional physical corruption (burst scratches, cosmic ray bit flips, transport packet loss) is repaired by Reed-Solomon. Active tampering beyond RS capacity fails AES-GCM authentication and is safely rejected.
  • Replay & Nonce Reuse Resistance: Master nonce combined with 4-byte block index counter guarantees that no two blocks share an IV.
  • Zero Silent Acceptance Guarantee: AES-256-GCM authentication tags prevent false-positive Reed-Solomon miscorrections from silently corrupting application state.
